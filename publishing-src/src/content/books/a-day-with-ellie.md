Ellie woke up before the sun.

Not because she wanted to — but because the big feeling had come back. The one that sat on her chest like a small, heavy stone. The one that didn't have a name yet.

She didn't wake her mother. She didn't wake her father.

She walked to the window and looked outside.

The world was still asleep.

And then — softly, quietly — something moved in the garden.

It was the dog. The big one. The one that always seemed to know things.

Ellie put on her shoes and went outside.

The dog didn't ask her what was wrong. The dog didn't try to fix anything. The dog simply sat beside her, warm and steady, and watched the sky slowly turn from grey to gold.

And somehow — slowly, quietly — the big feeling didn't seem so heavy anymore.

By the time the sun was fully up, Ellie knew three things for certain:

The feeling had a name.

The feeling was allowed to be there.

And the feeling was easier to carry — when it was carried together.
